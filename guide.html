User guide

Using Xiaomi Cockpit

This guide walks through the app from the first install to every setting. If you only want the short version, read Getting started; the rest is there when you need it.

Getting started

Requirements

Required for the car. A supported Xiaomi phone in the Chinese region, and Xiaomi interconnectivity that connects the phone to your car (with the same Xiaomi account on the phone and in the car).

  • A Xiaomi phone with HyperOS and Android 12 or newer, in the Chinese region.
  • A Xiaomi car that you can connect your phone to with Xiaomi interconnectivity.
  • Baidu Maps and Mi Dashboard must not be installed on the phone (see Troubleshooting).

Without interconnectivity the app can only be opened on your phone. It cannot be shown on the car screen. Everything in this guide that talks about the car needs the connection to work first.

Install the app

  1. On your phone, download Xiaomi-Cockpit.apk.
  2. Open the file. When Android asks to allow installing from your browser or file manager, allow it. If Play Protect warns about an unknown app, choose Install anyway.
  3. Open Xiaomi Cockpit. The first start is a guided setup: your language, your look (theme, accent colour, animated background and weather animation), the permissions and your map service (Google, Yandex or None; you can change it later in the settings). For Google you also read a short screen about Google's costs, accept it and add your key.
  4. Right after the permissions the setup asks Restore a backup? If you saved your settings before (see Backup and restore), tap Choose backup file: everything comes back and the setup ends there. Otherwise tap Skip and go on to the map service.

Tip. Installing a new version on top of the old one keeps all your settings, and the app can fetch updates for you (see Updating). The one exception is the move from version 2 to 3.0: see Moving to version 3.0.

If the install fails with a message about conflicting signatures or an existing package, Baidu Maps or Mi Dashboard (they use the same package name) is on the phone. Uninstall it and install again.

Connect to the car

Connect your phone to the car with Xiaomi interconnectivity (the same Xiaomi account on the phone and in the car). This connection is required: without it the app only opens on the phone. Then open Xiaomi Cockpit from the app list on the car screen, or pin it to the car's home screen. The app draws itself to fit the space the car gives it, and redraws if you resize it.

The first time you launch another app from the dock, HyperOS may show a Chain start question on the phone. Choose Always allow.

The three layouts

  • Phone and tall windows: clock, live map, music and the app row stacked from top to bottom.
  • Compact car screen: apps in a column on the left, music and clock side by side on top, the map as one wide box underneath.
  • Full car screen: apps on the left, a big map in the middle, the clock above a full music player on the right. From version 3.0 the car keeps this layout while you drive.

The layout is chosen automatically from the window's shape. Nothing scrolls, and sizes are scaled so that icons and text look the same size on the phone and on each car layout.

Clock and weather

The clock card shows the time in large light digits, the date on two lines, and the weather on the right: the temperature and an icon, the condition, your city and the day's high and low.

  • Weather source: Weather comes from Google (with your Google key) or from free Open-Meteo. It works with every map service; the Yandex key is only for the map. With Google the condition words (for example Cloudy) are Google's own; with Open-Meteo they are the app's. In Settings > Weather you find Weather provider, City, Weather animation and Update frequency (every 15 minutes to every 3 hours, 30 minutes by default). If Google does not answer, a note under the provider row says why and Open-Meteo is used for now.
  • City or your location: the City row opens a search: type a name and choose from the list, or tap Use my location. With no city chosen, the weather follows the phone's location by itself, and the place name comes from OpenStreetMap.
  • Next events (bottom left) lists up to three upcoming events, one per line, like Today · All day · Dentist or Tue · 14:30 · Team meeting. The first part is Today, then the weekday for the next days, and a date such as Oct 12 from a week away. Long titles are cut with “…”. It needs the Calendar permission. Tap the events to make the phone sync its calendars and read them again.
  • Status gauge (top right): the ring is the battery level, the Wi-Fi symbol inside shows Wi-Fi strength and the dots under it show mobile signal. When you plug the phone in, a green lightning bolt takes the place of the Wi-Fi symbol for a few seconds, and while it charges the ring is green and glows softly, with a pulse running along it.
  • Quick taps: tap the clock to switch between 12-hour and 24-hour time (all times in the app follow it), and tap the weather to refresh it now. Press and hold the weather to open the weather settings.

Weather effects

The dashboard shows the weather as well as telling it. The clock card has a sky with clouds that follow the condition (clear, partly cloudy, overcast, fog, rain, snow, storm). Rain falls over the whole dashboard, splashes on the top edge of the cards, and small drops form on the clock card, merge and run down the glass, bending the text behind them. Snow piles up on the top edges of the cards and the dock. Thunderstorms add lightning that flashes across the screen.

  • The weather never changes the theme or the map: Light and Dark stay as you set them, and the live map's night style is set with Night map (see Map and navigation). The weather picture on the clock card fades softly at the card's edge, like a view through glass.
  • The glass edge glow (the accent light along a card's rim) stays on top of the sky and the rain on the clock card.
  • The effects are on by default. Switch everything off with Settings > Weather > Weather animation. Then the theme stays as you chose it.
  • Rain and snow only show when the weather data is there, for your city or for your location.

Music

The music card shows what any music app is playing: the cover, the title and the artist, with previous, play / pause and next buttons.

  • When nothing is playing it says so; tap it to open your music app. Choose the app in Settings > Music > Default music app.
  • Like button: if the playing app offers a like (for example YouTube Music), a heart takes the place of the previous button. Tap to like or unlike.
  • Progress bar: the larger layouts show the elapsed time, the length and a bar you can drag to jump in the song.
  • Play when connected: optionally start playing by itself when the car connects (Settings > Look and behaviour).
  • Music ribbon: behind the buttons a twisting ribbon of thin lines, in the two main colours of the album cover, sways and turns with the music, with small dots rising from it. Faster songs make it move faster, and it calms down when the music pauses. The round buttons are frosted glass.
  • Cover: with nothing playing the cover is a frosted-glass tile with a disc icon, and a tap anywhere on the widget opens your default music app. Press and hold the widget to choose that app.
  • With the Album art accent, the colours of the app follow the cover of the current song.

Nothing shows? The music card needs Music and navigation access. Check that it says Allowed in the setup screen.

Map and navigation

The map is Google's own live map, with 3D buildings and traffic, following your car (or Yandex's map with traffic, if you choose the Yandex service). At night it switches to Google's dark map style. As you speed up it zooms out smoothly; while you drive it tilts into a driving view. Your car is a blue arrow. Tap the expand button to make the map fill the screen. On the phone and the compact car screen, the search and charger buttons first make the map bigger, and it goes back to its size when a route starts.

  • Driving view: always on. The map tilts into 3D by itself once the car moves.
  • Reset button: after you drag, pinch or turn the map, a round arrow brings it back to your position. It shows only after you moved the map.
  • Round buttons on the left of the map: Charger (bolt), Search, Voice (cycles spoken guidance between silent, alerts only and guidance, Google service only), View (3D navigation view or north up) and Settings (map settings such as live traffic and satellite view). The small map card shows only Charger and Search until you expand it. While a destination is chosen, an ✕ appears where the reset button sits; while a route runs, the trip buttons sit on the right (see Google's navigation screen). While you drive, the buttons fade away 5 seconds after you last touched the map and come back with a touch; the expand button always stays.
  • Night map (in the map's Settings button): Auto shows the night style while the sun is down where the car is, Theme follows the dashboard's theme, On always shows the night style of the map and Off the day style. The buttons and cards over the map change their colours to stay readable.
  • Bolt button (the first round button): finds nearby EV chargers, see Nearby chargers.

Choose the service in Settings > Service > API service (the setup guide asks on the first start):

  • Google (recommended): the live map, search, route options, Google's own turn-by-turn guidance (Navigation SDK) and weather, all with your own Google key.
  • Yandex: Yandex's live map with traffic, place search, nearby chargers and a route preview with Yandex's travel time, using your own free Yandex MapKit key. There is no turn-by-turn banner, because Yandex's free licence does not include guidance. The weather still works; the Yandex key is only for the map. See Get a Yandex key.
  • None: only the live map with a compass and your position. There is no search, route or turn-by-turn, and the weather comes from Open-Meteo. The search button asks you to choose an API service first.

Get a Google key

Routes, search, traffic times, turn-by-turn guidance, the chargers' details and Google's weather use your own Google API key, so you never use anyone else's allowance. One key does it all. Google gives each service a free monthly allowance and charges your own Google account above it, and the app asks for your consent before it uses anything that can cost money. The key stays on your phone. The live map itself is free. Pricing and allowances are Google's and can change, so check them in Google Cloud.

  1. Open console.cloud.google.com and sign in.
  2. Create a project and add a billing account (Google asks for a card, but the free monthly allowance is large).
  3. In APIs & Services > Library, turn on Map SDK for Android, Map Tiles API, Navigation SDK, Places API (New), Roads API, Routes API and Weather API.
  4. In APIs & Services > Credentials, create an API key and copy it.
  5. In the app open Settings > Service > API status and add the key (or add it in the setup guide), paste it and tap Check.

Check tries each Google service with your key. If all work, the button becomes Save. If something fails, the app tells you which one and what to fix (for example "Not turned on: press Enable in Google Cloud"). You can also choose Save anyway. The status row shows Valid (green), Needs fixing (red), Not checked or Not added (orange).

Consent. The first time you use Google, a glass screen explains the free allowances and the charge above them and asks you to accept. The acceptance is written to a consent log on your phone. In API status you can withdraw your consent: after a confirmation that removes the key from the phone and the Google services stop.

Tip. If you restrict the key to Android apps in Google Cloud, enter the app's package name (com.baidu.BaiduMap from version 3.0) and each SHA-1 fingerprint shown in the setup's Google key guide (there are two, each with a Copy button). A key without restrictions is simpler but anyone who gets it can use it, so keep it private.

Restricted your key before version 2.3.0? The app now has a new signing key, so it has a second SHA-1. Add the second SHA-1 to your key's Android restriction in Google Cloud. The old one keeps working, so nothing stops in the meantime.

Restricted your key before version 3.0? Version 3.0 has a new package name. Add com.baidu.BaiduMap with both SHA-1 fingerprints to your key's Android restriction in Google Cloud, or routes, search and guidance stop working.

Get a Yandex key

The Yandex API map service needs your own free Yandex MapKit key.

  1. Open yandex.com/maps-api/console and sign in (create a Yandex account first if you do not have one).
  2. Tap Connect on MapKit SDK. A key is created for you; copy it.
  3. In the app open Settings > Service, choose API service > Yandex and add the key in the setup guide or in API status, then tap Save. The map reloads with the new key by itself.

The Yandex key (API status) row shows Valid (green) once the map has loaded with the key, Not working yet (red) if it has not loaded, and Not checked or Not added (orange). A newly created key can take some time, sometimes 15 minutes or more, before the map loads. Once the key is valid, the How to get a key row goes away.

Set a destination

  1. Tap the magnifier (Search). A card opens at the top with Home, Work and a search field. Tap the field to type; a tap always brings the keyboard back.
  2. Type a place or an address. Results appear while you type, nearest to your car first. Tap one.
  3. The destination card shows the time and distance with Start, the alternative-routes button and + to add a stop. The route is drawn on the map, coloured by traffic: blue is normal, orange is slow and red is a jam.
  4. To stop, tap the ✕ button on the map: while a destination is chosen it takes the place of the reset button, and while a route runs it is one of the round buttons on the right, above the expand button.
  5. Alternative routes: the other routes are drawn on the map in grey. Tap one to choose it; a small bubble says how many minutes faster or slower it is. The route you choose is the one Google guides you along, even when it is a little slower. If you turn onto another road, a new route is found at once.
  6. Stops: tap + to add up to four stops. Tap the trip card to edit the trip: drag the stops into order or remove them. Each stop is drawn on the map as a numbered red disc.

With the Yandex API service the route is drawn with Yandex's traffic colours and travel time (a bubble shows the minutes), but the app does not guide you turn by turn.

To set Home and Work, open Settings > Map and tap the row. You can pick on the map, paste a map link (from Google Maps, Apple Maps, Waze, Amap, OpenStreetMap and others), type an address, or clear it. A spot picked on the map is exact.

Route overview. When you pick a destination while the car stands still, the map first zooms out to show the whole way, clear of the cards. It zooms in and follows you once you start driving or when you touch the map. Re-routing does not zoom out again. If you leave the route, guidance finds a new one at once.

Live traffic. The traffic colours stay on the route while Google guides you, and they refresh as you drive. Settings > Map > Live traffic update chooses how often: every 30 seconds, 1 minute, 2 minutes (the default) or 5 minutes. With Google each refresh is one Routes API request on your key; with Yandex the travel time refreshes too.

Your usual routes

The app learns the way you usually drive. It records your drives on your phone only (a point about every 50 metres, the last 200 drives) and never sends them anywhere. After two or more drives from about the same start to the same place, the route you usually take is shown first as Your usual route, and on the alternative routes on the map it is marked Usual.

  • It can only choose among the routes Google offers. If your usual way is not one of them, the normal order stays.
  • Turn it on or off with Settings > Map > Route learning (on by default). Turning it off deletes the learned drives.
  • Settings > Diagnostics shows how many drives were learned (Learned drives).

Google's navigation screen

While you drive a route with the Google service, the map becomes Google's own navigation screen: Google's blue arrow shows your car, Google's camera follows you (the View button chooses the 3D view, the top view or north up) and the route line shows the traffic. Google's own cards sit in frosted glass on top of the map:

  • Turn banner (top left): the turn arrow, the distance to the turn and the instruction with the street name. It is only as wide as its text needs; a long name goes onto a second line.
  • Then banner: when another turn follows closely, Google's small Then banner with its arrow sits in its own glass just below.
  • Arrival card (bottom left): the time left, the distance left and the arrival time.
  • Trip buttons (right, above the expand button): ✕ ends the route, the alternatives button shows other routes and + adds a stop. After you moved the map, a re-center button appears above them.
  • On the car screens the cards are sized to match the rest of the dashboard.

If you leave the route, a new one is found at once. Turn-by-turn needs the Google service: Yandex and None show no banner.

Nearby chargers

Tap the bolt button, the first of the round buttons on the live map. Green charger pins appear around you and the nearest charger is chosen at once. A card shows its name, the distance and drive time, every connector type known for the station (like CCS2 · GB/T) and where it is, smallest area first, for example Nirouth · Chbar Ampov · Phnom Penh. A part that is not known is simply left out.

  • The 15 closest chargers are found, wherever they are (there is no distance limit, so in a remote area they can be in the next district or province), with the quickest to reach first.
  • A bar at the bottom steps through the other stations with arrows. The map zooms to show you, the charger and the blue route between, and the other chargers as pins.
  • Go starts the route to the chosen charger. The ✕ where the reset button sits cancels your choice.
  • With your Google key the list comes from Google Places with the connector types; without a key, or when Google finds nothing, it comes from OpenStreetMap, which often has less detail (a station may then show no connector types).
  • While you drive a route, the bolt button hides the route for a moment and the card says Add: the charger becomes a stop before your destination and the route is recalculated. If you cancel, the old route comes back.

Phone notifications

With Notification access allowed, a frosted glass card slides in at the top of the dashboard when your phone gets a notification, so you can see it on the car screen.

  • Swipe the card up, or tap outside it, to dismiss it. Touches do not go through the card to the map below.
  • Turn it off in Settings > Look and behaviour > Show phone notifications on the dashboard.
  • The text is read on the phone only and is never sent anywhere.

The app dock

The dock holds your favourite apps. Tapping one opens it on the same screen the dashboard is on, so on the car screen the app opens on the car.

  • All apps: the last button opens a sheet with every app on the phone.
  • Edit the dock: press and hold the dock. The icons wobble slowly. Drag to reorder, tap the round badge at the top right of an icon to remove it, and tap the + button at the end to add apps (the list has search and app icons). Tap outside the dock, or open All apps, to finish.
  • Work profile and dual apps: the list of apps also shows the apps of your work profile and Xiaomi's dual apps, with their badge or marked (second copy). They open on the car screen like any other app.

Floating return button

When you open another app on the car screen, a small round glass button stays on top. Tap it once to come back to the cockpit; it blinks while it works and can take a second or two.

  • Move it by dragging it anywhere on the screen. It stays where you drop it.
  • It fades when you are not using it.
  • Turn it on or off in Settings > Look and behaviour > Floating home button. It is off by default, and it is never shown on the phone's own screen.
  • A silent notification keeps the button alive. To hide it, use Hide the floating button notification in the settings; the button keeps working.

Tip. The system back swipe on the car screen also returns you, even without the button.

Looks and themes

  • Theme: Follow phone, Dark, Light or Auto (sunrise/sunset), which is dark between sunset and sunrise at your weather city. The dashboard switches by itself, with a smooth fade, when the phone changes mode, when the sun sets or rises, or when you change the setting. Your choice always wins: the weather never changes it.
  • Background: the colour styles Cockpit, Dusk, Forest, Graphite and Slate, or My photo from your gallery. A press and hold on an empty spot of the dashboard opens the picker too.
  • Animated background: soft moving light and large colour bubbles behind the glass (not used with a photo).
  • Accent colour: Mint, Sky, Violet, Pink, Orange, Yellow, Red, Lime, a custom colour, or Album art to follow the song.
  • Weather animation (in Settings > Weather): the sky, rain, snow and lightning. Weather animation override, just below it, shows any condition, for example snow, even when the real weather is different.

  • Play music when connected to car, Show phone notifications on the dashboard, Floating home button.
  • Language: English, Khmer, Russian or Polish.

Troubleshooting

The app does not appear in the car's app list

First check the requirements: a supported Xiaomi phone in the Chinese region and a working Xiaomi interconnectivity connection to the car. Make sure the phone and the car use the same Xiaomi account and that interconnectivity is connected. The car only lists apps it recognises, and Xiaomi Cockpit uses a package name for that reason; if Baidu Maps or Mi Dashboard is installed on the phone, remove it and install Xiaomi Cockpit again.

The floating button does not bring the dashboard back

Tap once and wait two seconds (taps while it works are ignored). Check that Display over other apps is allowed, Autostart is on, and Battery saver is set to No restrictions. As a fallback use the system back swipe.

A permission switch is greyed out

Open App info, tap the three dots at the top and choose Unlock permissions, then try the switch again.

Music or the track title does not show

Check Music and navigation access. Start a song in your music app and give it a few seconds. Some apps do not offer a like button; then the previous button stays.

The map says "Allow location to show the map"

Allow Location in the setup screen and, for the car screen, check that the phone has a GPS fix (go outside or near a window).

Home, Work or search asks for a Google key

These need your own key. Follow Get a Google key. If Check reports a problem, fix the item it names (usually turning on one of the APIs or adding a billing account).

The Yandex map is black or says it is not loading

A newly created Yandex key can take 15 minutes or more before it works; wait a little and look at Yandex key in Settings > Service > API status. Check the internet connection and that the key was copied completely. If it stays red, create the key again in the Yandex console (Get a Yandex key).

"Finding the route…" never finishes

Check the internet connection on the phone and the key status in Settings > Service > API status. Press Check again to see which service fails.

The weather is empty

Choose your city in Settings > Weather, or leave it empty (or tap Use my location) and allow Location so the weather follows your position. Check the phone has internet. If you chose Google and it does not answer, a note under Weather provider says why (often the Weather API is not turned on for your key); the app then uses Open-Meteo.

Nearby chargers shows nothing, or too few

Check the internet connection and that Location is allowed. Without a Google key the list comes from OpenStreetMap, which may know fewer stations in your area. With a key, make sure Places API (New) is turned on (Check in Settings > Service > API status tells you). Use Report in the settings while the card is open if it still shows nothing.

Play Store offers the Baidu app as an "update"

Xiaomi Cockpit uses a Baidu package name, and very old versions had a lower internal version number than the real Baidu app, so Google Play offered Baidu as an update. Current versions have a much higher number, so the prompt goes away. Do not accept that "update".

Routes and search stopped working after moving to 3.0

Your Google key is probably restricted to the old package name. In Google Cloud, open the key and add the package name com.baidu.BaiduMap with both SHA-1 fingerprints (the setup's Google key guide shows them with Copy buttons), then tap Check in Settings > Service > API status.

The update does not install

The first time, Android asks you to allow this app to install apps: tap Open settings, switch on Allow from this source, come back and tap Update now again. If the download fails, check the internet connection. You can always download the newest APK from the Updating section and install it over the old one.

Something else is wrong, or it still does not work

Use Report a problem in the settings. Add a screenshot and keep the log on, then it is much easier to find the cause.

The layout looks too big or too small on the car

The app scales each car layout so it matches the phone. If it still looks off on your car, send a photo of the screen and the car model so it can be tuned.

Report a problem

If something does not work, you can tell the developer from inside the app. You do not need a GitHub account.

  1. Open the settings (swipe down on the dashboard, tap the gear) and find Support.
  2. Tap Report next to Report a problem. For an idea, tap Request next to Request a feature instead; it works the same way.
  3. Describe what went wrong and what you expected. Add up to three screenshots from your gallery with Add. Keep Include the app log on: it helps a lot. If the app crashed, the crash is included automatically.
  4. Tap Review. The next screen shows exactly what will be public. Scroll it to read everything.
  5. Tap Send. You get a report number and an Open on GitHub button.

What is public and what is private

  • Public: your description, the app version, the phone model and Android version, which layout was used, the state of the map and route services, and the last lines of the app log. Everyone can read this on the public list of reports.
  • Private: your screenshots and the full log. Only the developer can open them.
  • Removed before sending: your Google key, the Home and Work addresses, positions and e-mail addresses. Screenshots are not edited, so avoid showing personal details in them.
  • One report per minute can be sent. If sending fails (no internet, for example), the app offers to share the text through your phone's share sheet instead.

Following your report: open the link from the Open on GitHub button, or look for the number on the reports page. Answers and the fix are written there, and the fixed version is listed in the release notes.

Updating

The app can update itself. Open Settings > Updates and tap Check for updates: it looks at the project's published releases, shows what is new and, if you agree, downloads the new version and asks Android to install it over the old one (the first time Android may ask you to allow installing from this app). Check for updates automatically (on by default) does the same check by itself on the phone, at most every 12 hours, and asks before it installs anything. Your settings, key, Home and Work are kept.

Update channel: Stable (the default) only offers published releases. Dev also offers the newest test build, made automatically from the latest work; it can have bugs, and a newer stable release is still offered when there is one. Dev builds install over the stable app and keep your settings. If you switch back to Stable, you keep the dev build until a stable release newer than it comes out. The dev build can also be downloaded by hand from the dev pre-release on the releases page.

You can also download the newest Xiaomi-Cockpit.apk and install it over the old one. Your settings, key, Home and Work are kept. The releases page lists what changed in each version.

Moving to version 3.0

Version 3.0 uses a new package name, the one the car allows to stay full screen while you drive. Because of that it installs as a new app next to version 2 and does not update it; the old app's update check says the update is for a different app. Move once, it takes a few minutes:

  1. In the old app open Settings > Backup > Back up settings, choose With my API keys and save the file, for example in Google Drive or Downloads.
  2. Uninstall the old Xiaomi Cockpit.
  3. Uninstall Baidu Maps and Mi Dashboard if they are still on the phone. The new app cannot be installed next to them.
  4. Install the new Xiaomi-Cockpit.apk.
  5. In the setup, on Restore a backup?, tap Choose backup file and pick the file. The app restarts with all your settings, the dock, Home and Work and your keys. Allow the permissions again when the setup asks.
  6. If your Google key is restricted to Android apps, add the package name com.baidu.BaiduMap with both SHA-1 fingerprints in Google Cloud.

Backup and restore

Save all your settings in one file and bring them back later, for example on a new phone or after you install the app again. Open Settings > Backup.

Back up settings

  1. Tap Back up settings.
  2. Android's file picker opens. Save the file anywhere you like, for example in Google Drive or in your Downloads folder.

The file holds all your settings, the dock, Home and Work, your recent places, your background photo and the learned routes. Permissions are not in it: Android asks for them again after a restore.

The file is encrypted and always includes your Google and Yandex keys; only Xiaomi Cockpit can open it. Backups made with version 2 restore too.

Keep the file private. The file contains your Google and Yandex keys. It is encrypted, but do not share it.

Restore settings

  1. Tap Restore settings and pick the backup file.
  2. Confirm. The settings in the file replace the current ones, and the app restarts by itself.

On a new install you do not even need to open the settings: right after the permissions the setup asks Restore a backup? Tap Choose backup file to restore everything and finish the setup at once, or Skip to continue with the map service.
